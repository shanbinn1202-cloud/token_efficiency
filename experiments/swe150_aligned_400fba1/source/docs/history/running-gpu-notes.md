## 4.4 Several sessions on one server

On the GPU, 16 sessions ran in job 3339019 (`full`, `observation_query` and `random_fill`, eager, runtime `afffd2b`) and job 3343069 (`observation_query`, eager and with `--cuda-graphs full`, runtime `613a7d5`): synthetic sessions of 6 turns with 2,600-token observations, prompts up to about 14K tokens, 24 GiB at 16-token pages, on the reused clean environment of job 3270721 and one GH200. All 16 sessions decoded together, nothing was preempted, no turn waited for capacity, and an offline replay of every selective turn reproduced the recorded pages. Two eager runs of this workload (`observation_query` in both jobs) gave the same tokens on 72 of 96 turns: with batched decode a reply can change with the batch composition, which depends on timing. These contexts stay far below the 65,536-token limit, so the runs do not reach the limits described next.

The never-deadlock rule cannot hold for 16 `full` sessions at 64K on one GH200. They need about 67,350 attention blocks of 1 MiB plus the Mamba pool, about 70.6 GiB of KV, while the GPU has 94.53 GiB free at start (vLLM log of job 3313802) and the weights take 51.1 GiB. The selective policies need less only between rebuilds, since a rebuild returns to full residency. With a fixed `--active-token-budget 8192` at 16-token pages, 15 idle sessions holding their budget and their last turn's pages, plus one turn's peak, fit in 24 GiB when turns are about 7,000 tokens and need 34 GiB when they reach the 16,384-token observation limit; a 40 % budget needs 41 and 51 GiB on the same two assumptions. Size `--kv-cache-gib` and the workload accordingly. `CAPACITY_WAIT` holds a turn back while running requests can still free blocks. When nothing else runs, a turn that the idle sessions' pins leave no room for waits too, until a session closes or, with `--release-idle-pages` (section 4.13), idle sessions' pages are released; once every session waits, the server stalls. Before commit `68d19b4` (tb-capacity-fix, on integrate-1016; in main since integrate-1018) the gate admitted such a turn because nothing ran: on the GPU its decode took the last free block and vLLM preempted it, which is fatal (Slurm job 3398129 at runtime `0d37346`: 16 Terminus 2 trials, `full`, 32 GiB, the idle pins held all but 69 of 26,698 attention blocks). When no other session pins blocks, the turn is admitted as before (as on a one-task server). The largest `--kv-cache-gib` stays 32: with the Goal rewriter, about 36 GiB of the GPU is estimated to be left for KV (not measured).

On the GPU (job 3343069, runtime `613a7d5`): 24 tasks with K = 8, `observation_query` with `--cuda-graphs full-decode`, 6 turns of 3,000-token observations per task, 24 GiB at 16-token pages. Every task opened, ran its 6 turns and was closed by the harness with a close receipt (24 opens, 24 closes); at most 8 sessions ran at once, nothing was preempted, no turn waited for capacity, the offline selection replay matched every selective turn, and both pools were back at their readiness baseline at shutdown. The Mamba pool held 17 slots (2K + 1): K sizes it, not the 24 tasks (table above).

## 6. Harness interface

The launcher's `--protocol` accepts `mswea-xml` (the default), `kv-session` and `chat-text`. `mswea-xml` reproduces the dram-backed-kv PoC server token for token and is the protocol for the SWE-bench runs (sections 6.1 to 6.5). `kv-session` is the token-level session protocol of the MemoryAgentBench harness (section 6.6). `chat-text` is plain OpenAI chat text for agents such as Terminus 2 on Terminal-Bench 2.0 (section 6.7). `--enable-thinking` turns thinking on for `mswea-xml` and `chat-text` on the Qwen profile (opt-in, section 6.8; not validated: the keep mode ran on the GPU only in the `--goal-overlap` check of job 3500604). With it, `--thinking-history strip` serves `chat-text` the history of the stock default template, the reasoning dropped (opt-in, section 6.9; functional GPU checks passed in jobs 3493649, 3500646 and 3499759, no full benchmark yet).

## 6.1 Mini-SWE-Agent XML protocol

- **Why thinking off.** Every benchmark of this runtime runs with thinking off (thinking on is opt-in, section 6.8; on the GPU it has run only in functional checks: `chat-text` with the history of section 6.9 in jobs 3493649, 3500646 and 3499759, and the keep mode on `mswea-xml` in the `--goal-overlap` check of job 3500604), aligned with the PoC: its server (`scripts/serve_online_selector_v2.py` of dram-backed-kv) renders with `enable_thinking=False`, because LiteLLM / Mini-SWE-Agent remove the thinking block from the assistant content before the next request. With thinking on, the next request would no longer extend the KV prefix that the server holds (prefix-unstable, so no KV reuse); with thinking off, the empty think block is part of the prompt that the server renders (the generation header above), and the history stays append-only. A baseline run with thinking on is therefore not comparable with these runs.

## 6.7 Chat text protocol (Terminal-Bench 2.0 with Terminus 2)

`--protocol chat-text` serves plain OpenAI chat text on `/v1/chat/completions` from a server-owned token transcript, for agents that send their whole text history every turn and parse the reply themselves. The target is Harbor 0.21.0's Terminus 2 on Terminal-Bench 2.0, unchanged: JSON actions, no system message, thinking off. Such a server needs a session pool (`--max-running-sessions K`, section 4.4; 1 to 24 on a chat-text server, 1 to 16 on every other) and takes no `--task`: its registered tasks are slots that bind lazily to client sessions (`--chat-text-slots N`, 1 to 1024, default 512). It serves all eight policies; `goal_query` serves Harbor 0.21.0's Terminus 2 only, with Goal inputs that are **provisional** (below). `/v1/sessions/close` and the kv-session routes are refused. A release of the Gemma 4 profile serves the protocol with Gemma's turns (section 4.14): everything below holds for both models except the rendering, the stop ids and the capacity figures, `goal_query`'s Goal inputs included (the same provisional rules and record; gemma-tb-goal; on Gemma its Terminal-Bench dry run passed in job 3434544 after an offline re-verification, the job's own verdict being FAIL from harness checks: Status below).

## 6.9 Thinking on with the reasoning dropped (`--thinking-history strip`)

`--thinking-history strip` (opt-in, with `--enable-thinking`; Qwen3.6-27B with `--protocol chat-text` only) serves the history the stock default template renders: `enable_thinking=True` without `preserve_thinking`, every past reply as its content only, whatever reasoning the client sends back (the Terminal-Bench Vanilla baseline's input). **Functional GPU checks passed, all at runtime `8ca8511` with `chat-text` servers: jobs 3493649 (`THG1_PASS_VERIFIED`), 3500646 (`THG1B_PASS_VERIFIED`: the token differences against stock vLLM were CUDA-graph near ties; the analysis of one more reading is open) and the Terminal-Bench dry run 3499759 (`TB16_PASS_VERIFIED`, `observation_query` and `goal_query`). No full benchmark yet.** Without it stage 1's keep mode applies (section 6.8) and every launch, startup config, evidence byte and response is unchanged. Refused with `mswea-xml` and `kv-session`, (in this first version) with `--gdn-device-handoff` and `--phase1-in-step`, and with `--policy recent_tools` and `akv_rule`, whose reply classes cannot be read from a stripped history (section 5). On the Gemma 4 profile (gemma-thinking, CPU-tested only) the transcript side is in (every past reply `<|turn>model\n` + its content + `<turn|>\n`, the stock thinking-on render) and `--async-scheduling` is refused with it; its engine side holds the sliding window at the cut on the GPU ([docs/thinking-gemma/DESIGN.md](../thinking-gemma/DESIGN.md) section 4): `--window-hold-slots H` (1 to the running sessions; left out, the most that fit, and the server is refused when not even one fits) reserves H hold slots of one window each (812.5 MiB at 16-token Pages). A strip turn takes a hold slot from its admission to its commit (phase 1, a goal_query admission parked at its Goal and a cold prefill included), so at most H strip turns are in flight at once and the others wait at the capacity gate: with `--admission-overlap K` at most min(K, H) admissions are in flight and a Goal batch holds at most H calls (at H = 1 the server runs one turn at a time). The default leaves pool 0 at the selective rule's minimum, so a smaller H can serve more concurrent long contexts (the design note, section 4.3); H is pinned as `kv_layout.hold_slots` and printed by the dry run (`window_hold`). Not run on a GPU. Design and decisions: [docs/thinking-cut/DESIGN.md](../thinking-cut/DESIGN.md) (option (a), form E); the rules are in `remote_ra_live_v2/thinking_cut.py`.
